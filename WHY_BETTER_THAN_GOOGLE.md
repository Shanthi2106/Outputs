## Real-World Comparison

### Scenario: Parent Asks "What is a BIP?"

#### Google AI Response:
> "A Behavior Intervention Plan (BIP) is a written plan that teaches and reinforces positive behaviors. It's based on a Functional Behavior Assessment (FBA) and includes strategies to reduce problematic behaviors and teach appropriate replacement behaviors."

**Problems:**
- Doesn't explain what parents should DO
- No guidance on what makes a good BIP
- Doesn't explain the FBA connection clearly
- No red flags mentioned

#### This Assistant's Response:
> **Quick Answer:** A BIP is your child's 'behavior playbook' - it explains what triggers challenging behaviors, why they happen, and exactly what everyone should do.
>
> **What This Means for Your Child:** If your child has challenging behaviors at school, you should see a BIP in their IEP. It should be specific enough that any adult knows exactly how to respond.
>
> **Critical Point:** A good BIP focuses on teaching REPLACEMENT behaviors, not just stopping bad ones.
>
> **Questions to Ask:**
> 1. What is the function (purpose) of this behavior?
> 2. What replacement behavior will we teach?
> 3. How will you know if the plan is working?
> 4. Will this plan be followed consistently across all settings?
>
> **Red Flag:** A BIP that only lists punishments without teaching new skills usually doesn't work long-term.
>
> **Example:** If your child hits when frustrated, the BIP should teach requesting breaks using a break card, with staff immediately honoring those requests. This teaches a replacement skill (using the card) instead of just punishing hitting.

**Why This is Better:**
- ✅ Actionable information
- ✅ Empowers parents to evaluate quality
- ✅ Specific questions to ask
- ✅ Real example they can relate to
- ✅ Explains red flags
